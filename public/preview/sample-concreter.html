No guessing what's under the surface.

Two driveways can look the same on day one and last very differently. These are the details that decide it, and every quote lists them.

1

Thickness

The industry guide's minimum is 100 mm for cars under 3 tonnes, and 150 mm if heavier vehicles use it now and then.

On your site: the thickness you pour.

2

Concrete strength

Measured in MPa. Most home paving uses at least 20 MPa, with a higher grade for heavier loads.

On your site: the grade you use.

3

Reinforcement and joints

Steel mesh holds any cracks tightly closed, and saw-cut joints give cracking a straight line to follow.

On your site: your mesh and joint spacing.

4

What's underneath

Topsoil out, soft spots dug out, and road base compacted. It's the part you never see, and the part most shortcuts happen in.

5

Fall and drainage

A slope so water runs off, away from the house, and doesn't pond on the surface after rain.

6

Curing and when to drive on it

Concrete needs to stay moist while it gains strength: at least 3 days, and 7 within 1 km of the coast. Your quote says when it's safe to drive on.

On your site: your real curing and keep-off times.

Questions people ask.

Are you licensed?

Yes. Our licence number is at the top of this page and on every quote, and you can check it on the state's public register. On your site: your real licence number and a link to the register.

Do I need council approval?

Usually for the strip between your boundary and the kerb, because it's council land with its own rules for levels and thickness. We'll check your council's rules and tell you before we quote.

When can I drive on my new driveway?

It depends on the mix, the weather and what you drive. Your quote says how long to keep cars off, and we'll remind you on the day.

What if it rains or it's really hot?

We'll move the pour if we need to. The industry guide calls for extra precautions when it's over 30°C or under 10°C, and heavy rain can damage a fresh surface. In summer, we start early.

Will it crack?

Concrete shrinks as it dries, so fine cracks can happen. Saw-cut joints give it a straight line to follow, and mesh holds any cracks tightly closed. Your quote shows both.
